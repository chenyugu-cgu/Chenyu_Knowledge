# Preface

During my studies in biomedical engineering, bioengineering, and related engineering sciences, I have encountered a wide spectrum of knowledge: mathematics, physics, computing, control theory, and specialized topics such as mechanics, robotics, and signal processing. Each course or research experience has contributed valuable insights and techniques that proved essential at the time. Yet, as is often the case in an intensive academic journey, once a course ended or a project concluded, much of that knowledge tended to fade from immediate recall.

This book was born out of the need to **preserve, organize, and revisit** these lessons systematically. It is not simply a set of lecture notes, but a **cookbook of scientific methods and engineering principles**, curated to serve as both a learning tool and a long-term reference. The aim is to capture the concepts I have studied — from the foundational mathematics of calculus and probability, to the applied engineering fields of statics, dynamics, strength of materials, and control systems — in a form that emphasizes **clarity, applicability, and continuity**.

The structure of this book reflects both breadth and depth:

- **Mathematical and computational foundations** provide the essential tools.  
- **Core engineering sciences** build on those tools to model, analyze, and design systems.  
- **Signals, systems, and control** serve as a bridge between abstract theory and practical engineering implementation.  
- **Advanced domains** such as optimization, machine learning, robotics, and biomechanics connect fundamental knowledge to modern applications.  
- Finally, **cookbook-style recipes** present worked examples in a step-by-step format, so that each concept is reinforced by practice and context.

This book is written for myself, but also for anyone who shares the same struggle of *remembering what matters*. It is meant to be **scientific in content** yet **practical in form**, a bridge between textbook rigor and everyday usability. By collecting and systematizing the knowledge that might otherwise be lost, I hope to make it available not only for review but also as a **living foundation** that can grow with new insights, discoveries, and applications in the years to come.
