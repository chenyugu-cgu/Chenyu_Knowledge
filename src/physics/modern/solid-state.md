# Solid-State Physics

Solid-state physics explains how the quantum behavior of electrons in periodic crystals produces the electrical, thermal, and optical properties of materials — the basis of all electronics.

## Energy Bands

In a crystal, atomic energy levels broaden into **bands** separated by **gaps**. Electrons fill bands up to the **Fermi level**. The gap between the filled **valence band** and the empty **conduction band** classifies materials:

| Material | Band gap | Conductivity |
|---|---|---|
| Conductor | none (overlap) | high |
| Semiconductor | small (~1 eV) | tunable |
| Insulator | large (>5 eV) | negligible |

This band picture is why [semiconductors](../../eng/electrical/semiconductors.md) can be switched and doped.

## Carriers and Doping

Thermal energy promotes electrons across the gap, leaving **holes**. Doping with donors (n-type) or acceptors (p-type) controls carrier type and density, the essence of transistor and diode operation.

## The Free-Electron and Band Models

The free-electron (Sommerfeld) model with Fermi–Dirac statistics explains metallic conduction, heat capacity, and the Fermi energy. Bloch's theorem (electrons in a periodic potential) refines this into band structure.

## Thermal and Optical Properties

- **Phonons** (quantized lattice vibrations) carry heat and limit conductivity.
- **Optical absorption** turns on above the band gap — why silicon is opaque to visible light but transparent to infrared, and how photodetectors and solar cells work.

## Beyond Basic Semiconductors

Superconductivity (zero resistance below a critical temperature), magnetism, and the engineered band structures of quantum wells and dots extend the field into modern devices, including those used in [medical imaging](../../app/biomed/imaging.md) detectors.
