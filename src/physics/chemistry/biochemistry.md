# Biochemistry

Biochemistry is the chemistry of life — the molecules and reactions that build and run cells. It is indispensable for biomedical engineering: drugs, biomaterials, biosensors, and tissue engineering all act at the biochemical level.

## The Major Biomolecules

| Class | Building block | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Proteins | amino acids | structure, enzymes, signaling |
| Nucleic acids | nucleotides | information (DNA, RNA) |
| Carbohydrates | monosaccharides | energy, structure |
| Lipids | fatty acids/glycerol | membranes, energy storage |

Protein **structure** (primary → secondary → tertiary → quaternary) determines function; misfolding causes disease.

## Enzymes and Kinetics

Enzymes are protein catalysts that accelerate reactions by lowering activation energy. Their kinetics follow the **Michaelis–Menten** model:
\\[
v = \frac{V_{\max}[S]}{K_M + [S]},
\\]
where \\(K_M\\) is the substrate concentration at half-maximal rate. This same saturating form recurs in receptor binding and [pharmacokinetics](../../app/biomed/drug-delivery.md).

## The Central Dogma

\\[
\text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{transcription}} \text{RNA} \xrightarrow{\text{translation}} \text{Protein}.
\\]
Genetic information flows from sequence to function — the basis of molecular biology, genetic engineering, and [bioinformatics](../../app/biomed/bioinformatics.md).

## Metabolism and Energy

Cells extract energy by oxidizing fuels, storing it as **ATP**. Pathways (glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation) are governed by the same [thermodynamics](physical.md) and kinetics as any chemistry, but exquisitely regulated.

## Relevance to Biomedical Engineering

- **Biosensors** detect specific biomolecules (antibodies, enzymes, DNA).
- **Drug design** targets proteins and pathways; binding and clearance follow [pharmacokinetics](../../app/biomed/drug-delivery.md).
- **Biomaterials** must be compatible with the biochemical environment ([Biomaterials](../../app/biomed/biomaterials.md)).
- **Tissue engineering** controls the biochemical cues that direct cell growth.
