# Biomaterials

A **biomaterial** is any material designed to interface with biological systems — implants, scaffolds, drug carriers, and devices. The defining requirement beyond mechanical performance is **biocompatibility**.

## Biocompatibility

A biomaterial must perform its function without provoking an unacceptable host response. Considerations:
- **Toxicity** — no harmful leachables.
- **Immune/inflammatory response** — minimize chronic inflammation and rejection.
- **Thrombogenicity** — blood-contacting materials must resist clotting.
- **Degradation** — controlled (resorbable) or stable, as intended.
- **Sterilizability** — survive sterilization without degrading.

The body is a harsh, warm, saline, dynamic environment — materials that thrive in air may corrode or fatigue inside it.

## Classes of Biomaterials

| Class | Examples | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Metals | Ti alloys, CoCr, stainless steel | orthopedic/dental implants, stents |
| Ceramics | alumina, zirconia, hydroxyapatite | joint surfaces, bone scaffolds |
| Polymers | PMMA, PEEK, silicone, PLGA | lenses, sutures, drug delivery |
| Composites | carbon-fiber, fiber-reinforced | prosthetics, dental |
| Natural | collagen, chitosan | scaffolds, wound care |

## Property Matching

A key design principle is **mechanical matching**. Bone is far less stiff than metal; a too-stiff implant carries load the bone should bear, causing **stress shielding** and bone resorption. This drives interest in lower-modulus titanium alloys, PEEK, and porous structures that approach bone's modulus.

## Surface Engineering

The surface, not the bulk, meets the tissue. Coatings and treatments tune the interface:
- **Hydroxyapatite / bioactive coatings** promote bone bonding (osseointegration).
- **Surface texturing** guides cell attachment.
- **Anti-fouling / drug-eluting coatings** reduce infection and restenosis.

## Tissue Engineering

Scaffolds (often resorbable polymers like PLGA) provide a temporary 3-D template for cells to grow new tissue, degrading as the tissue forms. Porosity, degradation rate, and mechanical cues must be co-designed — a frontier blending materials, biology, and [mechanics](../biomechanics/joints.md).
