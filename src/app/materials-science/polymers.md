# Polymers and Composites

Polymers and composites dominate modern lightweight design — from packaging to aircraft wings. They achieve properties unattainable by any single conventional material.

## Polymers

Polymers are long-chain molecules built from repeating monomer units. Their behavior depends on chain structure and temperature.

### Classification

- **Thermoplastics** (PE, PP, PET, nylon) — soften on heating, reshapeable, recyclable.
- **Thermosets** (epoxy, phenolic) — cross-linked networks; rigid, heat-stable, not remeltable.
- **Elastomers** (rubber, silicone) — lightly cross-linked, large reversible strains.

### The Glass Transition

Below the **glass-transition temperature** \\(T_g\\), an amorphous polymer is hard and brittle (glassy); above it, soft and rubbery. \\(T_g\\) is the single most important polymer design temperature. Semi-crystalline polymers also have a melting point \\(T_m\\).

### Viscoelasticity

Polymers are **viscoelastic** — their stiffness depends on time and temperature, exhibiting creep, stress relaxation, and hysteresis. Modeled with spring–dashpot networks (Maxwell, Kelvin–Voigt); time–temperature superposition lets short high-temperature tests predict long-term behavior.

## Composites

A composite combines a **matrix** (binds, transfers load) with a **reinforcement** (carries load): e.g. carbon or glass fibers in an epoxy matrix.

### Rule of Mixtures

For aligned fibers loaded along their axis, the modulus is the volume-weighted average:
\\[
E_\parallel = V_f E_f + (1 - V_f) E_m,
\\]
while transverse loading gives the much lower
\\[
\frac{1}{E_\perp} = \frac{V_f}{E_f} + \frac{1-V_f}{E_m}.
\\]
This **anisotropy** is the whole point — strength is placed where it is needed.

### Why Composites Win

- **High specific strength/stiffness** (per unit weight) — critical for aerospace and sports equipment.
- **Tailorable** — fiber orientation and stacking sequence tune directional properties.
- **Corrosion- and fatigue-resistant.**

Trade-offs: cost, difficult joining and repair, complex failure modes (delamination, fiber pull-out), and recycling challenges.
