# Materials Science

Materials science studies the relationship between a material's **structure**, **properties**, **processing**, and **performance** — the four corners of the field's central paradigm. Choosing and engineering materials is fundamental to every branch of engineering.

## The Structure–Property Paradigm

\\[
\text{Processing} \longrightarrow \text{Structure} \longrightarrow \text{Properties} \longrightarrow \text{Performance}.
\\]
A material's behavior is dictated by its internal structure across scales — from atomic bonding, to crystal arrangement, to microstructure (grains, phases), to bulk form. Controlling processing controls structure, which controls properties.

## Classes of Materials

| Class | Bonding | Typical traits |
|---|---|---|
| Metals | metallic | ductile, conductive, tough |
| Ceramics | ionic/covalent | hard, brittle, heat-resistant |
| Polymers | covalent + van der Waals | light, flexible, insulating |
| Composites | mixed | tailored, high specific strength |
| Semiconductors | covalent | tunable conductivity |

## Length Scales

- **Atomic** — bonding type sets stiffness and melting point.
- **Crystal** — lattice and defects govern strength and conductivity. See [Crystal Structures](crystals.md).
- **Microstructure** — grains, phases, and their boundaries control mechanical behavior.
- **Macroscale** — bulk shape and the engineering [properties](properties.md) we measure.
