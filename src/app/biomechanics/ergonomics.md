# Ergonomics and Injury

Ergonomics (human factors) designs tasks, tools, and environments to fit the human body — reducing injury, fatigue, and error while improving comfort and performance. Injury biomechanics studies how mechanical loads damage tissue.

## Why It Matters

Poor ergonomics causes **musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs)** — back pain, carpal tunnel, tendinitis — among the most common and costly workplace injuries. Good design prevents them and improves productivity.

## Biomechanical Risk Factors

- **Force** — high muscle/joint loads (lifting, gripping).
- **Posture** — awkward or static positions away from neutral.
- **Repetition** — cumulative loading without recovery.
- **Vibration** — hand-arm and whole-body vibration.
- **Duration** — sustained exposure.

These map directly to the muscle and joint forces computed in [musculoskeletal modeling](musculoskeletal.md).

## Assessment Tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| NIOSH lifting equation | recommended weight limit for lifts |
| RULA / REBA | rapid posture scoring |
| Anthropometry | body-size data for design (percentiles) |

The NIOSH equation, for example, scales a load constant by factors for horizontal/vertical distance, asymmetry, and frequency to give a safe lifting limit.

## Injury Biomechanics

Injury occurs when load exceeds tissue tolerance — a fracture, ligament rupture, or, at high rates, [tissue failure](tissue.md). Injury biomechanics establishes **tolerance thresholds** (e.g. head injury criteria, spinal compression limits) used to design seatbelts, airbags, helmets, and protective equipment, often validated with crash dummies and simulation.

## Design Principles

- Design for the **range** of users (anthropometric percentiles), not the average.
- Keep work in **neutral postures** and within comfortable reach.
- **Reduce force** with mechanical aids; **vary tasks** to avoid repetition.
- Apply to workplaces, vehicles, consumer products, and [assistive devices](../biomed/rehab.md).
