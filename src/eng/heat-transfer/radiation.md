# Radiation

Thermal radiation transfers heat by electromagnetic waves and needs **no medium** — it crosses vacuum. Every body above absolute zero radiates.

## The Stefan–Boltzmann Law

A surface radiates power
\\[
q = \varepsilon\sigma A T^4,
\\]
with \\(\sigma = 5.67\times10^{-8}\\) W/m²·K⁴ and **emissivity** \\(\varepsilon\in[0,1]\\) (1 for a perfect black body). The fourth-power dependence makes radiation dominant at high temperatures (furnaces, the Sun, incandescent filaments).

## Black Bodies and Spectra

A black body absorbs and emits perfectly. **Planck's law** gives its spectral distribution; **Wien's displacement law** locates the peak:
\\[
\lambda_{\max} T = 2.898\times10^{-3}\ \text{m·K}.
\\]
Hotter bodies glow at shorter wavelengths (red → white). This is the physics behind infrared thermometry and thermal imaging — including medical thermography.

## Surface Properties

By Kirchhoff's law, a good emitter is a good absorber (\\(\varepsilon = \alpha\\)). Real surfaces are characterized by emissivity, absorptivity, reflectivity, and transmissivity, which depend on wavelength and angle. Selective surfaces exploit this (solar collectors, low-e coatings).

## Radiation Exchange

Net exchange between two surfaces depends on temperatures, emissivities, and the **view factor** \\(F_{12}\\) (the fraction of one surface's radiation reaching the other). For a small object in a large enclosure:
\\[
q = \varepsilon\sigma A(T_s^4 - T_{surr}^4).
\\]

## Applications

Spacecraft thermal control (radiation is the only mode in vacuum), furnace and combustion design, infrared sensing and thermography, solar energy, and building energy efficiency.
