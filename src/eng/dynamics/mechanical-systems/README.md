# Dynamics of Mechanical Systems

Undergraduate dynamics mostly treats particles and planar rigid bodies. Real machines are three-dimensional. Gyroscopes precess, wheels roll without slipping, linkages carry moving frames on moving frames, and constrained systems need more than \\(F = ma\\) to write their equations of motion. This subsection develops three-dimensional kinematics and kinetics systematically. It then introduces the analytical methods (Lagrange's equations, constraint formulations, and Kane's method) that turn a mechanism into a set of equations a computer can integrate.

It follows the graduate course *Dynamics of Mechanical Systems* (Georgia Tech ME 6441), built on Ginsberg's *Engineering Dynamics*.
